In consumer apps, bad design is obvious: people simply leave. In business-to-business (B2B) software, users often *have* to use the tool, so the cost of poor design is quieter — but it is still paid every day.

## Where the costs hide

### Lost time

When a common task takes ten clicks instead of three, the extra seconds repeat across every employee and every working day. Confusing navigation and unclear labels add up to hours each month.

### Training and onboarding

Software that needs a long manual to use is expensive to roll out. New staff take longer to become productive, and experienced staff spend time teaching workarounds.

### Errors and rework

Forms without validation, ambiguous buttons and missing confirmations lead to wrong data. Fixing mistakes later is far more expensive than preventing them in the interface.

### Support tickets

Every "how do I…?" question is a support cost. Clear design reduces ticket volume and frees your team for higher-value work.

### Lost deals and renewals

Buyers increasingly try software before purchasing. A clumsy demo or trial can lose a deal to a competitor with fewer features but a better experience.

## Practical ways to improve

1. **Watch real users.** Five short sessions observing people complete key tasks will reveal the biggest problems.
2. **Design for the most frequent tasks.** Put the actions people use daily within one or two clicks.
3. **Use a consistent design system.** Shared components keep buttons, forms and messages predictable.
4. **Write clear microcopy.** Labels, error messages and empty states should say exactly what to do next.
5. **Measure.** Track task completion time, error rates and support volume before and after changes.
